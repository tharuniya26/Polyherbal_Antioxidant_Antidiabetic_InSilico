# Molecular target selection and antioxidant computational strategy

**Project:** Integrated in-vitro and in-silico investigation of five polyherbal aqueous leaf extracts  
**Stage:** Literature and structure metadata review, 11 October 2026  
**Status:** Candidate targets selected for subsequent receptor assessment. No protein coordinates were downloaded, no docking was run, and no antioxidant quantum calculations were performed.

## Research context and interpretation boundary

The original study combined aqueous leaf extracts of *Aegle marmelos*, *Boerhavia diffusa*, *Senna auriculata*, *Salacia reticulata*, and *Vitex negundo* in the proportions recorded in [`config/formulations.yaml`](../../config/formulations.yaml). Its glucose-related measurements were **glucose adsorption and yeast glucose uptake**, not alpha-glucosidase or alpha-amylase inhibition assays. Docking to the human digestive enzymes below would therefore test a new, plausible mechanism; it cannot explain the existing glucose-assay observations without new experimental evidence. Likewise, the reported DPPH and ABTS results concern mixtures, and none of the shortlisted individual compounds was identified in the original research extracts.

The present [shortlist](../../data/compounds/compound_shortlist.csv) contains mangiferin and (−)-epicatechin from published *Salacia reticulata* water-leaf studies. The [evidence register](../../literature/compound_evidence.md) distinguishes this independent botanical evidence from the original project extracts. Their chemical form, particularly the stereochemistry absent from connectivity-only SMILES, still needs checking before ligand preparation. The eight other candidate associations remain on hold.

## Why these antidiabetic-related enzymes?

Human pancreatic **alpha-amylase** cleaves internal alpha-1,4 glycosidic bonds in dietary starch, producing smaller oligosaccharides. Intestinal brush-border **alpha-glucosidases**, including maltase-glucoamylase (MGAM), release glucose from those oligosaccharides. Inhibition could delay carbohydrate digestion and glucose appearance. These are human physiological targets with experimental inhibitor-bound structures, but a docking pose or score alone is neither enzyme inhibition nor an antidiabetic effect. MGAM has separate N- and C-terminal catalytic domains with different substrate preferences; a single MGAM domain does not represent all intestinal alpha-glucosidase activity, including sucrase-isomaltase. [Sim et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2007.10.069); [Ren et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13238-011-1105-3); [Brayer et al., 1995](https://doi.org/10.1002/pro.5560040908).

RCSB lists the inhibitor in 1XD0 as [chemical component ARE](https://www.rcsb.org/ligand/ARE), explicitly named **acarbose derived pentasaccharide**. The [primary 1XD0 study](https://doi.org/10.1021/bi048334e) examines acarbose rearrangement by human pancreatic alpha-amylase. Redocking ARE would test recovery of *that deposited product pose*, not whether GOLD can reproduce binding of intact acarbose. For MGAM, the acarbose site is documented by the respective [N-terminal](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2007.10.069) and [C-terminal](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13238-011-1105-3) structure papers. The RCSB entries also show glycan, water, and crystallization components; those require explicit classification during preparation.

## Binding-site definition and validation feasibility

For 2QMJ, 3TOP, and 1XD0, the experimental inhibitor coordinates define a defensible binding-site center and a reference pose. The later docking protocol should select a documented chain, remove only the chosen reference ligand for redocking, retain a copy of its crystallographic coordinates, and verify ligand stereochemistry, bond order, protonation, and atom correspondence. Compare predicted and experimental heavy-atom poses using a defined RMSD approach, including chemically valid symmetry handling; inspect key catalytic contacts and report failure as well as success. A customary pose-recovery cutoff can be prespecified, but it is **not yet achieved**. For 1HNY, transfer a site definition by structural alignment with 1XD0 and assess receptor sensitivity; it cannot provide an independent native-ligand redocking test.

MGAM acarbose binding involves a carbohydrate-rich catalytic cleft; [RCSB PDB-101](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/global-health/diabetes-mellitus/drugs/alpha-glucosidase-inhibitors/drug/acarbose/acarbose) documents the NtMGAM acarbose contacts. For pancreatic alpha-amylase, the experimentally described catalytic residues **Asp197, Glu233, and Asp300** provide a site identity check, but the site boundary should come from the observed inhibitor and surrounding protein rather than residue names alone. [Brayer et al., 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8528071/). Inspect whether any unresolved atoms or alternate side chains touch the chosen site and whether chain A versus B in 3TOP differs materially.

CCDC GOLD preparation must address glycan stereochemistry and many polar/rotatable groups, ligand charge and tautomer assignments, catalytic residue protonation, crystallographic water choices, and carbohydrate-specific hydrogen-bond networks. The amylase entries include calcium and chloride; their removal needs a structural and functional justification. Bound N-acetylglucosamine in 2QMJ/1XD0 reflects glycosylation rather than the candidate inhibitor. GOLD permits ligand-based site definition and limited side-chain flexibility, but it does not make the receptor backbone fully flexible; a predeclared receptor and water sensitivity assessment is appropriate. Its scores should be compared only within a common validated protocol and never translated directly into inhibition constants. [CCDC GOLD documentation](https://www.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/solutions/software/gold); [CCDC pose-recovery guidance](https://support.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/support/solutions/articles/103000306441-why-is-it-not-possible-to-return-the-crystallographic-binding-pose-of-the-ligand-).

**Decision:** retain 2QMJ and 1XD0 as first structures for later preparation; retain 3TOP to test the second MGAM domain and 1HNY as an apo amylase comparison. This is target selection, not authorization to treat the receptors as docking-ready. If ligand extraction, active-site completeness, or pose recovery fails, reconsider the entry or define the limitation before screening phytochemicals.

## Antioxidant-related computational analysis

### Descriptors: a structural screen

Record verified chemical identity and stereochemistry, phenolic O–H count and position, catechol/other conjugated motifs, glycosylation, molecular size, polarity, and plausible ionization states. These descriptors can support a qualitative radical-scavenging *plausibility* discussion. They do not supply reaction energetics, rates, DPPH/ABTS inhibition values, or proof of activity. PubChem connectivity-only SMILES should not be treated as a complete stereochemical input for (−)-epicatechin.

### Quantum chemistry: a possible later mechanistic study

Phenolic radical-scavenging pathways can include hydrogen atom transfer (**HAT**), single-electron transfer followed by proton transfer (**SET–PT**), and sequential proton loss followed by electron transfer (**SPLET**). A later quantum-chemical protocol could estimate O–H bond dissociation enthalpy for HAT; adiabatic ionization potential and proton dissociation enthalpy for SET–PT; and proton affinity/deprotonation free energy plus electron-transfer enthalpy for SPLET. It would need optimized neutral, radical, radical-cation, and relevant anionic states, appropriate spin/charge assignments, a consistent electronic-structure level, solvation and pH assumptions, conformer sampling, and validation against suitable reference molecules. Orbital energies or a single HOMO–LUMO gap alone do not establish any of these pathways or an experimental rate. [Phenolic mechanism study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2210271X12002198).

The planned **Avogadro** workflow can create and inspect molecular geometries and interfaces with quantum chemistry programs, while **Discovery Studio Visualizer** serves structure inspection and **GOLD** serves protein–ligand docking. Avogadro's default force-field optimization is not a DFT calculation. Quantitative HAT or electron-transfer energetics require a separately selected and validated quantum-chemistry engine and protocol; none is specified in the current Python requirements. A simple descriptor analysis is feasible without that engine. [Avogadro overview](https://avogadro.cc/); [Avogadro optimization documentation](https://avogadro.cc/docs/tools/autoopt-tool.html).

**DPPH and ABTS are radical-scavenging assays, not protein targets.** Docking a phytochemical to alpha-glucosidase, alpha-amylase, or an unrelated antioxidant-associated protein does not reproduce chemical quenching of DPPH or ABTS. Any eventual computed reaction energetics must be interpreted against the assay's solvent, radical identity, concentration, kinetics, pH, and mixture effects; formulation-level responses cannot be assigned to an individual shortlisted molecule.
