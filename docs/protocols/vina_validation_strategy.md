# AutoDock Vina validation strategy for the antidiabetic target models

**Status:** literature and structure-record assessment, 12 October 2026. No native-ligand redocking or production phytochemical docking has been run. A receptor that parses in Vina is not thereby chemically or physically validated.

## Scope and evidence used

This plan evaluates human pancreatic α-amylase and the **N-terminal catalytic domain** of human intestinal maltase-glucoamylase (ntMGAM). It uses the existing [1XD0 input preflight](vina_1xd0_input_preflight.md), [Vina receptor blocker assessment](vina_receptor_blocker_resolution.md), [native-redocking plan](vina_native_redocking_plan.md), [target-selection record](molecular_target_selection.md), and their accompanying metadata CSV files. The requested `vina_1XD0_preflight.md` is not present; `vina_1xd0_input_preflight.md` is the existing report. Structure identities, resolutions, ligands and primary citations below were checked against the linked RCSB records. Ligand heavy-atom counts come from the RCSB chemical component records. The **estimated** rotatable-bond counts in [the candidate table](../../data/metadata/alternative_validation_complexes.csv) were calculated with RDKit 2026.03.6 `CalcNumRotatableBonds` from the RCSB component stereochemical SMILES; they are descriptors, not measured crystal torsions or a guarantee of easy docking.

The original aqueous-leaf experiments did not assay these enzyme targets. A plausible pose, redocking success, or a favorable Vina score would not establish inhibition, antidiabetic efficacy, or the presence of any library compound in those extracts.

The RCSB [MYC](https://www.rcsb.org/ligand/MYC), [0XR](https://www.rcsb.org/ligand/0XR), [MIG](https://www.rcsb.org/ligand/MIG) and [SSD](https://www.rcsb.org/ligand/SSD) chemical component records are the reference for atom names, connectivity, stereochemistry and heavy-atom counts. The primary papers support the inhibitor binding interpretation; a smaller ligand or a high-resolution model alone does not establish a valid docking benchmark.

**Selection:** Use 4GQR and 3L4W as separate target-specific validation candidates. There is no single receptor whose redocking result validates both enzymes. Among the alternatives, 4GQR has the strongest *deposited active-site pose* evidence and least ligand flexibility, while 3L4W avoids the known α-amylase calcium/chloride scoring problem. Neither is ready for a reported Vina validation run until its own receptor and ligand preparation gates pass.

## Status of the existing 1XD0 and 2QMJ models

| Existing structure | Defensible present role | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [1XD0](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1XD0) human pancreatic α-amylase | **Conditional sensitivity and comparative receptor model**; retain the unchanged originals and the audited all-water/site-water variants. | ARE is the deposited acarbose-derived pentasaccharide, not intact acarbose: 55 heavy atoms and 28 torsions in the current inputs. Both receptor PDBQTs passed Vina 1.2.7 `--score_only` parsing, but the existing preflight marks physical scoring blocked: Vina ignores input partial charges, treats Cl⁻ as hydrophobic chlorine and Ca²⁺ as a generic metal, and fixes crystallographic waters. ARE has crystal-mate contacts. Its neutral amine state and catalytic Asp197/Glu233/Asp300/His201 states are unresolved. The diagnostic scores are **not** affinities or validation. |
| [2QMJ](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/2QMJ) human ntMGAM | **Structural comparator and conditional sensitivity model**; no production Vina scoring claim. | The complete deposited α-acarbose is PRD_900007, reconstructed as one reference ligand. It remains highly flexible (44 heavy atoms, 22 torsions in the existing plan). The binding-site NAG A2005 and linked glycan chemistry could not be retained through a validated Meeko 0.8.0 receptor template; the existing no-NAG PDBQT is explicitly a sensitivity model. Catalytic Asp443/Asp542/His600 protonation, Gln837 completeness, and water/glycerol/sulfate decisions remain unresolved. |

Retain calcium and chloride in α-amylase structural interpretations and evaluate their coordinating environment. Omitting them solely to make Vina run would change the scientific model. PDBQT acceptance does not resolve their score contribution: [Vina's FAQ](https://autodock-vina.readthedocs.io/en/latest/faq.html) states that its standard scoring ignores user-supplied partial charges. If the chosen Vina scoring mode cannot represent a necessary retained species credibly, report that model as **physically blocked for score interpretation** and consider an independently validated metal/ion-aware workflow or different engine. A pose-recovery exercise may still be reported explicitly as a diagnostic, provided the ion limitation is carried through every result.

Crystallographic waters are fixed in ordinary rigid-receptor Vina calculations; deposited occupancy does not prove that all must be retained in solution. For each complex, inspect distances and hydrogen-bond geometry, alternate positions, and contact to the reference ligand. Predefine site-water and justified dry/wet sensitivity variants, recording every retained or omitted water by residue/atom identifier. Do not silently strip glycans or PCA. Distinguish linked NAG from separate crystallographic NAG, and verify any custom templates against the deposited linkage and stereochemistry. Review catalytic acid/base and histidine microstates in each receptor independently; preparation software defaults are hypotheses rather than evidence.

## Reproducible validation experiment — future work

### 1. Freeze and qualify inputs

1. Retrieve the selected entry's official RCSB mmCIF/PDB and CCD definition; record entry version, URL, SHA256, experimental resolution, chain, mutation, missing residues/atoms and alternate locations. Preserve deposited coordinates and reference ligand atom names. Select one inhibitor instance where the entry contains several.
2. Prepare **each complex independently**. Validate the bound inhibitor's complete graph, stereochemistry, charge and crystallographic pose. Record all receptor atom additions, protonation choices, glycans, PCA, ions and waters. Inspect the catalytic site and crystal mates. Confirm heavy-atom coordinates have not drifted unexpectedly and that no steric conflict was introduced. Test actual Meeko/PDBQT conversion and Vina parse; then assess physical scoring suitability separately.
3. Decide ligand microstates and receptor protonation at a stated pH, retaining alternate plausible states as labeled sensitivity cases. In particular, evaluate miglitol's amine, MYC phenols, ARE amine, catalytic acids and histidines. Do not infer protonation solely from the absence of crystal hydrogens.

### 2. Define search and run controls

4. Define the box center as the centroid of the selected *deposited native inhibitor heavy atoms*. Compute its coordinate extrema and add at least 5 Å margin on each axis initially; record exact center/size in Å and inspect that the whole ligand and relevant subsites fit. Avoid unnecessarily large boxes; increase size only for a documented reason.
5. Make independent starts from randomized translation, rotation and appropriate conformers **away from the crystal pose**. Preserve chemical identity and stereochemistry; never use a minimized crystal pose as the sole starting state. Keep a copy of the untouched reference pose solely for RMSD. Run at least five prespecified seeds (for example 1101, 2202, 3303, 4404, 5505) with the same Vina 1.2.7 executable, scoring mode, protonation state, box and receptor variant. Start with exhaustiveness 32, `num_modes` at least 20, and report `energy_range`; repeat at 64 and, if needed, 128 for a **search-convergence check**, particularly for carbohydrate references. Record input/output hashes, command lines, threads, runtime and all failed or incomplete runs. These are proposed settings, not performed calculations.
6. Redock the native ligand against **its own** prepared receptor. Do not treat 4GQR redocking as validation of 1XD0, or 3L4W redocking as validation of 2QMJ. If later cross-docking is attempted, align receptor structures first and label it a separate transferability test.

### 3. Pose comparison and interpretation

7. Read docked poses back with a validated PDBQT parser. Match heavy atoms to the deposited ligand using the CCD atom-name-to-graph map and explicit stereochemistry. Compute the *minimum* RMSD over chemically equivalent graph automorphisms where appropriate; do not compare PDBQT line order or include hydrogens. In the same coordinate frame, calculate `sqrt(mean(||x_docked - x_crystal||²))` for mapped heavy atoms **without fitting the ligand to itself**. If structures are in different frames, superpose the receptor backbone first and apply that transform to the docked pose. Report any unmatched atoms as a failure to calculate a meaningful RMSD.
8. Report top-ranked pose RMSD, best RMSD among all saved modes, pose rank, fraction of independent seeds recovering the native site/pose, run-to-run spread, and whether higher exhaustiveness changes those outcomes. A heavy-atom RMSD near or below 2 Å may be used as a conventional pose-recovery *screen*, with an explicitly stated threshold, but is not proof of physical scoring accuracy or biological inhibition. Report failed runs and alternative-site poses; do not select only the best seed after seeing the result.
9. As positive controls, use each complex's own experimentally bound inhibitor and, where compatible, an independently documented inhibitor of the same human target. For negative controls, use compounds shown inactive or substantially weaker in a comparable biochemical assay and with comparable preparation; do not label an untested phytochemical a negative control. Keep decoys and score distributions separate from native-pose recovery, and do not compare raw Vina scores across receptor types or protonation variants as if they were affinities.

## Decision gate

**Diagnostic redocking may be attempted only after new 4GQR and/or 3L4W inputs pass identity, receptor-chemistry, microstate, PDBQT readback and binding-site checks.** No such inputs or redocking outputs were generated in this assessment. 4GQR is the preferred α-amylase test but the calcium/chloride limitation must be stated even if pose recovery succeeds. 3L4W is the preferred ntMGAM test if its N-linked glycan and catalytic site can be represented without unsupported deletion. If a necessary component remains unsupported, mark that receptor variant blocked, retain it as a documented sensitivity model if scientifically useful, and seek a validated alternative scoring method before interpreting scores or ranking the phytochemical library.
