# Native-ligand redocking validation plan for CCDC GOLD

**Status:** Reference chemistry, deposited heavy-atom poses and atom mappings have been checked for 2QMJ and 1XD0. This is a prospective validation protocol. **No GOLD run, docking score, pose RMSD or receptor validation result exists yet.** See the [receptor preparation record](receptor_preparation.md) and [machine-readable results](../../data/metadata/receptor_preparation_results.csv).

Both reference SDF files preserve the deposited **heavy-atom pose**. Their hydrogens are computationally generated. Keep those SDFs immutable as comparison references. Generate a **separate, independent input conformer** of each exact chemical graph and stereoisomer for redocking, with a recorded random seed and preparation method, so the native coordinates are not fed to GOLD as the initial ligand geometry. Multiple starts are appropriate for these flexible oligosaccharides. Check that every starting conformer has the official connected graph and assigned stereochemistry; reject any changed anomer, linkage, or ARE/acarbose substitution.

## Receptor and site preparation gates

1. Inspect the official structure validation reports and electron density around each inhibitor, catalytic residues, heteroatoms and nearby waters. Record any uncertain atoms or low-occupancy regions. Use an unchanged source and a separately named prepared variant.
2. For **2QMJ**, use protein author chain A; resolve alternate positions explicitly, assess missing construct residues 1–6 and 837, and review NAG A2005, each site water, glycerol and sulfate by geometry and relevance. The protein catalytic nucleophile/acid-base pair is Asp443/Asp542. For **1XD0**, use author chain A; preserve linked PCA A1 and initially preserve Ca A498, Cl A500 and Ca-coordinating HOH A536/A546; review other waters and NAG A499. The catalytic triad is Asp197/Glu233/Asp300.
3. Add receptor hydrogens with a declared preparation tool, version and pH assumption. Inspect histidine tautomers, Asp/Glu protonation, ion coordination, glycan linkage, atom valence and clashes. Record all water/ion/heteroatom additions or removals and their justification. Do not minimize deposited heavy atoms without a recorded, justified protocol and a retained original pose.
4. Import each separately prepared receptor and ligand into GOLD/Hermes; verify atom typing, bond orders, stereochemistry, charge, hydrogens, metal coordination and ligand flexibility. An SDF/PDB file extension alone does not establish usable GOLD chemistry ([CCDC format guidance](https://support.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/support/solutions/articles/103000306413-which-file-formats-does-gold-support-)).
5. Start binding-site inspection from the **heavy-atom geometric centroid** of the deposited reference: 2QMJ **(−20.808, −6.586, −5.074) Å**; 1XD0 **(10.441, 15.880, 40.721) Å**. In GOLD, choose and record the actual site definition and radius only after checking that it covers the full native inhibitor, catalytic residues and intended binding cleft without an unjustified surrounding volume. For 2QMJ, assess whether the distal sugars would make a single centroid/radius too broad. Save a site-coverage screenshot or coordinates and the final settings.
6. Predeclare GOLD version, scoring function, search efficiency, number of independent runs/poses, genetic-algorithm settings, ligand flexibility, water handling and any constraints. Keep validation settings separate from later exploratory phytochemical screening. Run only after the receptor gates above are satisfied.

## Heavy-atom pose comparison

The mapping CSVs give a **1-based SDF heavy-atom index**, component/residue, chain, author residue number and deposited atom name for each reference atom. Use this mapping to pair native-reference atoms with the redocked chemical graph; do not assume GOLD output atom order is unchanged. Verify the graph and stereochemistry of every compared output pose. Exclude computational hydrogens from the primary RMSD. Align on matching protein-chain heavy atoms if the receptor coordinate frame changed; do not align the ligand to itself to hide pose displacement. Calculate positional RMSD over the **full mapped ligand heavy-atom set** and document treatment of chemically equivalent/symmetry-related atoms. Retain a strict name-based result and, where graph automorphisms permit, a symmetry-corrected result. Report missing atoms or unequal graphs as validation failures, not as a smaller favorable RMSD.

Also report diagnostic RMSD by region: AC1, middle GLC and distal GLC separately for 2QMJ; catalytic-site and crystal-contacted O3A/C3A regions for 1XD0. The 2QMJ distal sugar has higher deposited B factors, and 1XD0 ARE contacts a crystal mate near O3A/C3A, so a full-molecule RMSD must be interpreted with these structural limits. Inspect catalytic contacts, clashes, stereochemistry, ring conformations, native ligand coverage and pose clustering. A commonly used pose-recovery threshold may be prespecified, but any pass/fail threshold must be chosen **before** viewing results and must not replace visual and chemical inspection. Record failures and all parameter changes. GOLD's own [pose-recovery guidance](https://support.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/support/solutions/articles/103000306441-why-is-it-not-possible-to-return-the-crystallographic-binding-pose-of-the-ligand-) notes that crystal-pose recovery depends on receptor and ligand preparation, search and scoring.

## Results record to create after an actual run

For each receptor and trial, save: prepared receptor and starting-ligand hashes; source and reference IDs; software/tool versions; pH, protonation and water/ion decisions; site coordinates and radius; GOLD configuration and random seeds; complete output poses and scores; full and diagnostic heavy-atom RMSDs with mapping method; stereochemical/graph checks; clashes and contact observations; and any failed runs. A score or a low RMSD would assess one protocol against one crystallographic inhibitor pose. It would not establish inhibition by the project's phytochemicals, the presence of those compounds in the original aqueous extracts, or reproduction of DPPH/ABTS radical scavenging.
