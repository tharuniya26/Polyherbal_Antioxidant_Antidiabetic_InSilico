# Phytochemical evidence corrections before ligand preparation

**Reviewed:** 11 October 2026  
**Scope:** Six existing shortlist associations and the linked 13-row expanded register. This is a source audit, not analysis of the original experimental extracts.

## Decision rule

**High-confidence botanical occurrence** requires a compound-specific analytical result in a primary paper using the named species, leaves, and water-only extraction. A PubChem CID verifies a reference structure, not its occurrence in an extract or the source paper's stereochemical assignment. **Conditional** means that at least one botanical, extraction, analytical-access, or isomer requirement remains unresolved. Both labels concern literature evidence for later *structure assessment*, not docking readiness or experimentally measured activity of an individual molecule in this project.

| **AM01, gallic acid — *Aegle marmelos*** | [Ahmad et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants10122573); leaf identification by HPLC-PDA. | The paper's methods extract authenticated leaves with **ethanol** and match gallic acid to a standard by retention time. A later total-phenolic passage calls the material “aqueous,” but does not overturn the explicit ethanolic preparation. | **BD01, caffeic acid — *Boerhavia diffusa*** | [Thomford et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules21020211) was recorded as verified water-leaf identification. | This is a CYP2B6/extract-interaction study whose exploratory UPLC-MS Table 4 lists caffeic acid. That report is **not accepted here as independently verified leaf-specific analytical proof**. A replacement primary analytical source, [Sinan et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox10122003), Table 1, identifies underivatized caffeic acid against a reference standard in a **boiled-water infusion of mixed aerial parts** (distribution code 4), not leaves alone. Retained for structure review with these limits. |
| **SR01, mangiferin — *Salacia reticulata*** | [Yoshino et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1271/bbb.80854) was cited for water-leaf HPLC identification, although its abstract describes biological effects. | Inspection of the **full primary paper** finds a methods subsection for HPLC determination of mangiferin in the water extract of *S. reticulata* leaves. Its results report mangiferin at about **0.62% of the leaf extract**. Thus the original DOI does contain compound-specific analytical evidence; no replacement citation is needed. The mouse glucose response cannot be assigned to mangiferin alone, and the reported HPLC does not independently establish every stereochemical detail of PubChem CID 5281647. | **High-confidence aqueous-leaf botanical occurrence;** molecular stereochemistry and activity attribution remain conditional. |
| **SR02, (−)-epicatechin — *Salacia reticulata*** | [Sekiguchi et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1186/1756-0500-5-198); hot-water leaf HPLC catechin analysis. | The methods boil the leaves in distilled water, and the results describe an epicatechin-labelled HPLC peak in the extract using a green-tea catechin control. No chiral chromatographic or optical-rotation verification of the exact **(−)** isomer was found. No replacement needed. | **High-confidence aqueous-leaf association at the epicatechin level;** CID 72276 stereoisomer selection remains conditional. |
| **SA02, quercetin — *Senna auriculata*** | [Nakamura et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1248/cpb.c14-00420); leaf isolation under the name *Cassia auriculata*. | The source uses **80% aqueous acetone** on leaves, followed by **ethyl-acetate fractionation** and isolation of quercetin. Water in a mixed solvent or chromatography phase is not water-only extraction. The source's cell assay concerns hepatoprotection. No replacement needed. | **Conditional:** direct organic-solvent leaf association; water-only occurrence and digestive-enzyme activity unverified. |
| **VN03, agnuside — *Vitex negundo*** | [Katare et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42250-022-00383-8) was labelled direct aqueous-leaf evidence. | The publisher abstract names leaf agnuside, quantitative HPLC, qualitative HPLC-MS/MS, and an **aqueous** solvent comparison. Independent [Shah et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpha.2013.09.008) identify agnuside in leaves, but their “aqueous extract” was prepared by **first extracting leaves with methanol**, then partitioning into water. | **Conditional:** aqueous-leaf comparison reported in publisher abstract; full water-only compound-specific data not independently checked. |

## Linked expanded-register corrections

**BD03 (p-coumaric acid)** was added using the same Thomford CYP2B6 paper. Its former direct-aqueous-leaf label has been removed and it is **on hold** because no independent compound-specific water-leaf replacement was verified. **VN04 (negundoside)** was added using the Katare paper. Its label is now **aqueous-leaf report in publisher abstract only**; the complete water-specific analytical data remain inaccessible, and the compound remains on hold. These two rows remain in the expanded register. BD02's previously documented parent-versus-TMS-derivative problem remains on hold.

The Sinan paper cannot close BD01's leaf-only gap: its starting material is explicitly **aerial parts**. The original Thomford table is recorded as an exploratory report, not erased from the provenance trail. The PubChem CIDs and isomeric SMILES already present in the two CSV files were preserved; this correction has not independently revalidated each PubChem structure against the primary analytical method.

## Counts and next evidence checks

The shortlist still contains **six associations: two high-confidence aqueous-leaf botanical associations (SR01 and SR02) and four conditional associations (AM01, BD01, SA02, VN03)**. These are six distinct named structures in the shortlist. The expanded register retains **13 associations**; no original candidate or experimental result was deleted.

The most useful next source checks are a leaf-only water-extract chromatogram or LC-MS/MS identification for BD01, full water-specific analytical data for VN03 and VN04, and stereochemical verification for SR02 and BD01 if exact stereoisomers are to be modelled. Direct chemical profiling of the project's original aqueous leaf extracts would be needed to establish their actual constituent identities. None of these literature reports demonstrates that an individual candidate was present in the original formulations or caused their antioxidant or glucose-related assay results.
