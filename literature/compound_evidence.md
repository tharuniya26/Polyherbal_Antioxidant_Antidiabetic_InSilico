# Evidence register for preliminary phytochemical candidates

**Scope:** Ten plant–compound associations (nine distinct chemical structures) for the five species used in the original aqueous leaf-extract study. This register records what independent publications report. None of these compounds has been analytically confirmed in the original project's extracts. The original report, chromatograms, and raw extract data are not deposited in this repository.

## How to read the evidence

- **Species and leaf evidence** means a cited primary study analyzed leaves of the named species, including the accepted synonym *Cassia auriculata* for *Senna auriculata*.
- **Aqueous leaf evidence** requires that the named molecule was detected in a water or hot-water leaf extract. An aqueous *chromatography mobile phase* or aqueous organic solvent does not meet this criterion.
- **Activity evidence** is separate from compound occurrence. An active crude extract or fraction does not prove that an identified molecule caused the activity.
- PubChem CIDs and connectivity SMILES in the CSV identify reference structures only. Connectivity SMILES omits stereochemistry and double-bond geometry; the isomeric PubChem record and reported chemical identity must be checked during later structure assessment.

The separate [shortlist](../data/compounds/compound_shortlist.csv) contains only SR01 and SR02. Shortlisting authorizes review of chemical identity, stereochemistry, and structure records; it is **not** approval to dock or a claim of biological activity in the original formulations.

## Outstanding verification

Eight of ten rows lack cited water-only leaf detection: six have other-solvent leaf evidence (Class B), while two additionally rely on putative underivatized parents inferred from derivative GC-MS peaks (Class C). The two *Salacia* rows have published water-leaf evidence (Class A), still not confirmation in the original project material. Before any candidate becomes a final ligand, recheck the source's analytical identification, exact chemical form and stereochemistry, aqueous extraction relevance, and intended computational mechanism. All ten require direct chemical profiling of the original extracts to establish actual presence there.
