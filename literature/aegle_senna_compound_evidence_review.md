# Evidence review: *Aegle marmelos* and *Senna auriculata* candidate phytochemicals

**Reviewed:** 11 October 2026  
**Scope:** Seven requested plant–compound associations (six distinct chemical structures). This review supports an exploratory literature library; it is **not** a chemical profile of the project's original aqueous leaf extracts. The existing compound datasets have not been changed.

## Evidence standard and taxonomy

**Tier A** requires direct compound-specific identification in a **water-only leaf extract**. **Tier B** requires identification in leaves extracted with a different solvent, including an aqueous organic mixture or a fraction prepared from one. **Tier C** means species-level evidence exists but leaves or extraction details cannot be established. **Unverified** means the available source cannot support a reliable species–compound association. A compound used only as an HPLC standard, a TPC/TFC equivalent, or an antioxidant assay comparator is not an identified constituent.

[Kew Plants of the World Online](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A770819-1) accepts *Aegle marmelos* (L.) Corrêa. Kew lists [*Cassia auriculata* L. as a synonym of the accepted *Senna auriculata* (L.) Roxb.](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A484161-1). The synonym justifies linking the *Cassia* papers to *Senna*; it says nothing about which plant part was analyzed.

For the [Food Chemistry paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodchem.2010.08.065), the publisher abstract and accessible article preview support the aerial-part and isolation statements; complete experimental details were not accessible. For the [Pharmacognosy Journal paper](https://doi.org/10.5530/pj.2018.3.75), the publisher abstract explicitly specifies flowers and names the screening method; it is not used as leaf evidence.

## Recommendation for the next selection decision

For a small exploratory modelling set, **gallic acid, quercetin, kaempferol, and luteolin** have traceable leaf evidence and comparatively straightforward reference structures. **Rutin** and **kaempferol-3-O-rutinoside from *Senna*** have sufficient botanical evidence for an optional glycoside comparison, with additional conformational and stereochemical preparation work. **The *Aegle* kaempferol-3-O-rutinoside assignment remains conditional at the exact-isomer level** despite the reported leaf LC-MS/MS peak; it should not be promoted to a stereodefined ligand solely from formula and aglycone fragments.

**No candidate reaches Tier A in this review.** All seven associations have at least a published leaf assignment under **Tier B**, but the *Aegle* kaempferol rutinoside assignment is more tentative than the other six. The two papers sometimes invoked as broader support for *Senna* add species-level context only: one starts from mixed aerial parts and the other from flowers. Extraction solvents used as LC mobile phases, standard solutions, or post-extraction fractions cannot substitute for water-only leaf extraction. Antioxidant and antidiabetic effects of crude extracts or fractions do not establish an individual molecule's mechanism or presence in this project's five-plant formulations.
