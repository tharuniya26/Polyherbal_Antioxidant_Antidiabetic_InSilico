# Five-plant phytochemical evidence review

**Review date:** 11 October 2026  
**Scope:** Reassessment of the ten original plant–compound associations and a focused search for additional named molecules in *Aegle marmelos*, *Boerhavia diffusa*, *Senna auriculata*, and *Vitex negundo*. The original *Salacia reticulata* pair is carried forward for comparison. This is an external-literature library, **not** a chemical profile of the project's aqueous leaf extracts.

## Evidence rules and data products

The [expanded register](../data/compounds/expanded_compound_candidates.csv) preserves every original ID and adds three separately numbered associations (BD03, VN03, VN04). It records **one row per botanical association**; AM02 and SA01 both refer to rutin, so a later ligand library needs only one prepared rutin molecule with two provenance links. The [five-plant preliminary shortlist](../data/compounds/five_plant_preliminary_shortlist.csv) contains six conditional structure-assessment priorities, including at least one from each plant. Inclusion means **review the molecular structure and modelling feasibility**, not that the candidate is in the original extract, binds a digestive enzyme, or is ready for GOLD.

| CSV category | Interpretation | Current IDs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `direct_published_aqueous_leaf` | The primary paper contains compound-specific analysis of water or hot-water leaves, including a reported result. | SR01, SR02 |
| `aqueous_leaf_report_abstract_only` | A publisher abstract names the compounds and compares an aqueous leaf extraction, but full water-specific data were not accessible for independent checking. | VN03, VN04 |
| `published_leaf_other_solvent` | Leaf identification uses ethanol, methanol, aqueous organic solvent, or an organic fraction. | AM01, AM02, SA01, SA02, VN01, VN02 |
| `species_level_nonmatching_part_or_solvent` | The named underivatized compound is analytically reported from the species, but the water-extracted material includes non-leaf aerial parts; separate leaf evidence involves a derivative after methanol extraction. | BD01 |
| `unverified_or_insufficient` | The exact parent structure or claimed aqueous-leaf identification has insufficient independent support. | BD02, BD03 |

“Aqueous” in a **mobile phase** or a **water fraction of a methanol-first extract** does not mean that leaves were extracted with water alone. `isomeric_SMILES` strings and CIDs were retrieved from [PubChem PUG REST](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/docs/pug-rest) on 11 October 2026. This verifies the *reference chemical records*, not that a paper resolved every stereocenter, double-bond geometry, or positional isomer. Rows with a structure/source mismatch risk are marked in `structure_verification_status` and `notes`.

## Plant-by-plant reassessment

### *Aegle marmelos* — two retained, no new inclusion

- **AM01, gallic acid:** [Ahmad et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants10122573) used authenticated leaves and **ethanol** extraction, then assigned gallic acid by HPLC retention-time comparison with a standard. PubChem [CID 370](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/370) gives an unambiguous non-stereogenic small-molecule reference. Its phenolic groups support an antioxidant-chemistry hypothesis, but the article's extract assays do not demonstrate gallic acid's individual contribution. **Conditional lower-confidence shortlist** for structure assessment; its ionization state and water-only leaf relevance need review.
- **AM02, rutin:** The same ethanol-leaf HPLC report provides a species/leaf association, corroborated by an independent [methanol-leaf LC-MS report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6320333/). PubChem [CID 5280805](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/5280805) is the same structure as SA01. Its multiple sugar stereocenters, polar groups, and flexibility complicate ligand preparation and score comparisons. **Hold**; no direct water-leaf identification was verified.

A [hydroalcoholic leaf HPTLC paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5757330/) uses an “aqueous fraction,” but extraction began with ethanol–water; this is not a water-only leaf study. A separate paper reports named chemicals from a water-leaf LC-MS workflow, including several synthetic medicines such as [gosogliptin and bortezomib](https://microbiologyjournal.org/antimicrobial-activity-against-antibiotic-resistant-pathogens-and-antioxidant-activity-and-lcms-ms-phytochemical-content-analysis-of-selected-medicinal-plants/). Without orthogonal confirmation or a credible provenance for those assignments, it was **not** used to add candidates. Total phenolic content expressed as gallic-acid equivalents is not gallic-acid identification. Thus no individual *A. marmelos* water-leaf candidate met the standard for promotion here.

### *Boerhavia diffusa* ? two retained; one added association remains on hold

- **BD01, caffeic acid:** The previous citation to [Thomford et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules21020211) was presented as verified water-leaf analytical proof. That paper is principally a CYP2B6 extract study; although its exploratory UPLC-MS Table 4 lists caffeic acid for *B. diffusa*, this correction review does **not** accept it alone as independently verified, leaf-specific confirmation of the exact modelled molecule. A replacement primary analytical study by [Sinan et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox10122003) identifies caffeic acid against a reference standard by UHPLC-HRMS in a **boiled-water infusion of mixed aerial parts** (Table 1, distribution code 4). Its material is not isolated leaves. [Juneja et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtcme.2019.02.002) used methanolic leaves and GC-MS after trimethylsilyl derivatization; Table 2 names a **tri-TMS derivative (CID 5376254)**. That observed derivative must not be equated silently with the listed underivatized PubChem parent [CID 689043](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/689043), and E/Z geometry remains unresolved. The plant association is plausible, but **water-only leaf occurrence is unverified**. Retain BD01 as a **conditional lower-confidence structure-assessment candidate**, not a high-confidence aqueous-leaf compound.
- **BD02, ferulic acid:** [Juneja et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtcme.2019.02.002) report a **di-TMS derivative (CID 5379186)** from methanolic leaves, whereas the register's [CID 445858](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/445858) is the underivatized trans parent. Parent identity, geometry, and water-leaf occurrence remain unverified; **hold**.
- **BD03, p-coumaric acid (added association):** Thomford et al. Table 4 also lists this acid, but that exploratory CYP2B6 paper is not being used as verified independent leaf-specific occurrence evidence. No replacement primary water-leaf analytical source was verified for BD03 in this correction review. Its prior direct-aqueous-leaf category has been removed and the row is **on hold**; the listed PubChem E-isomer has not been assigned experimentally in that paper.

Sinan et al. support an underivatized caffeic-acid association in a **water infusion of mixed aerial parts**, but the leaf-only question remains open. Their extract-level antioxidant and digestive-enzyme assays do not establish caffeic acid's individual activity. The original project's extracts have not been chemically profiled for this compound.

### *Salacia reticulata* ? two original water-leaf records retained

- **SR01, mangiferin:** The abstract of [Yoshino et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1271/bbb.80854) describes a biological study and by itself is not chemical identification. Inspection of the **full paper**, however, finds a methods subsection for HPLC determination of mangiferin in the **water extract of *S. reticulata* leaves** and a result of approximately **0.62% of the leaf extract**. This is compound-specific primary analytical evidence, so the direct aqueous-leaf category is retained; no replacement DOI is necessary. The HPLC result does not independently resolve all stereochemical details encoded by PubChem [CID 5281647](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/5281647). Mouse glucose effects belong to the whole extract, not isolated mangiferin. **High-confidence botanical occurrence for this evidence tier; conditional for future structure assessment.**
- **SR02, (?)-epicatechin:** [Sekiguchi et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1186/1756-0500-5-198) boiled leaves in distilled water and detected an epicatechin-labelled HPLC peak in that extract with a green-tea catechin control. This supports the compound class and leaf-water association. The accessible methods do not demonstrate chiral separation or optical rotation, so the exact (?)-stereoisomer represented by PubChem [CID 72276](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/72276) remains conditional. The study's cell proliferation assay is not an individual antidiabetic test. **High-confidence botanical occurrence at the epicatechin level; conditional stereochemical choice.**

### *Senna auriculata* — two retained, no new inclusion

- **SA01, rutin:** [Nakamura et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1248/cpb.c14-00420) isolated it from leaves named *Cassia auriculata*, a synonym of *S. auriculata* according to [Kew Plants of the World Online](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A518328-1). Extraction used **80% aqueous acetone**, followed by fractionation; this is not water-only evidence. It shares [CID 5280805](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/5280805) with AM02. **Hold** because water recovery is unknown and glycoside modelling is relatively demanding.
- **SA02, quercetin:** The same paper isolated quercetin from an **ethyl-acetate fraction** of aqueous-acetone leaves. The isolated aglycone has a clear [PubChem reference, CID 5280343](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/5280343), and is straightforward to inspect structurally, but its presence in water-only leaves remains unknown. **Conditional lower-confidence shortlist** to represent a plausible leaf molecule, with the solvent mismatch explicit. The source bioassay is hepatoprotection, not quercetin inhibition of the project's proposed digestive targets.

A primary [aqueous-leaf screening study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arabjc.2021.103345) measured phytochemical *classes* and total phenolics/flavonoids, whereas its GC-MS named compounds and alpha-amylase/alpha-glucosidase assays concern **methanolic leaves**. Gallic-acid and quercetin “equivalents” in that paper are calibration units, not identifications. A [metabolomics paper](https://doi.org/10.1089/omi.2021.0010) confirms distinct leaf, flower, and bud profiles, but accessible source details did not support a named **water-leaf** assignment suitable for inclusion here. No additional compound met the required identification and solvent standard.

### *Vitex negundo* — two retained; two additional water-leaf markers

- **VN01, chlorogenic acid:** [Wu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules29133133) identified and quantified it in leaves extracted with **80% aqueous methanol**. PubChem [CID 1794427](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/1794427) is a specific caffeoylquinic-acid isomer; the article also discusses other positional isomers, so a peak/standard check is needed. **Hold** for water-only occurrence and positional-isomer confirmation. Fraction antioxidant activity is not isolated-compound activity.
- **VN02, isoorientin:** The same aqueous-methanol leaf LC-MS/MS study supports its occurrence. PubChem [CID 114776](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/114776) is a stereodefined C-glycoside reference. **Hold** for water-only evidence and more demanding glycoside preparation. The paper's fraction responses do not identify the active individual molecule.
- **VN03, agnuside (added association):** The [Katare et al., 2022 publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42250-022-00383-8) explicitly names agnuside in *V. negundo* leaves and says quantitative HPLC and qualitative HPLC-MS/MS compared aqueous, ethanol, methanol, and 20%-ethanol-water solvent systems. It says the **20%-ethanol-water** system outperformed aqueous extraction; the optimized yield cannot be assigned to water alone. The full water-specific chromatogram, extraction conditions, and quantitative table were inaccessible, so the water-leaf identification is recorded as **abstract-level, conditional**. A separate [Shah et al. HPLC study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpha.2013.09.008) independently supports agnuside in *V. negundo* leaves but extracted those leaves with **methanol first**, then partitioned into water; that is not direct water extraction. PubChem [CID 442416](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/442416) is a reference structure, not proof of stereochemistry in the extracts. **Conditional shortlist**.
- **VN04, negundoside (added association):** The same [Katare et al. abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42250-022-00383-8) names negundoside and compares an aqueous system with organic solvent systems, but the water-specific analytical data were inaccessible. Its prior direct-aqueous-leaf label is reduced to **abstract-level, conditional**. PubChem [CID 9935561](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/9935561) is a reference structure. Its larger flexible glycoside remains **on hold**.


A [validated HPLC paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5761016/) reports agnuside in an “aqueous extract” of *V. negundo* leaves, but its method first extracts the leaves with **methanol** and then partitions that extract with water. It is independent support for botanical occurrence, **not** independent direct water-leaf evidence. An aqueous-leaf GC-MS report dominated by small esters/hydrocarbons did not provide a sufficiently robust phenolic or iridoid assignment for this modelling library.

## Modelling interpretation and remaining gaps

The six shortlisted associations comprise **two with full-paper compound-specific water-leaf evidence** (SR01, SR02), **one with aqueous-leaf evidence visible only in a publisher abstract** (VN03), **two with direct organic-solvent leaf evidence** (AM01, SA02), and **one with underivatized aqueous mixed-aerial-part evidence plus separate derivatized methanolic-leaf evidence** (BD01). The last four are conditional for botanical match to the original extraction. The expanded register retains 13 associations; BD03 is now on hold. Every PubChem CID and stored isomeric SMILES in the expanded register resolves to a PubChem chemical record; this is **reference-structure verification**, not full verification of the source's exact isomer. Before a GOLD ligand is prepared, review E/Z geometry for BD01/BD03, epicatechin optical assignment, chlorogenic positional isomer, glycoside stereochemistry, protonation of phenolic acids, and conformational sampling of large glycosides. A reference molecule should be modelled once even if linked to more than one plant.

**Botanical occurrence and bioactivity remain separate.** Phenolic groups motivate antioxidant-mechanism calculations, while the target-selection protocol makes digestive enzyme binding a testable hypothesis. Extract-level DPPH, ABTS, glucose, or enzyme responses cannot be assigned to one constituent. No candidate has been analytically identified in the original formulations, and the original aqueous-leaf extraction conditions and individual compound concentrations are unavailable. Direct LC-MS/HPLC profiling of those extracts is needed for that claim.

A **five-plant exploratory, evidence-tiered structure-assessment library** remains possible only if BD01, AM01, SA02, and VN03 are visibly treated as conditional literature candidates. There are **two high-confidence aqueous-leaf botanical associations and four conditional shortlist associations**; none is confirmed in the original experimental extracts. A **five-plant library claimed to represent the original aqueous extracts is not yet defensible**, because *A. marmelos* and *S. auriculata* still lack verified named water-leaf molecules here, and none of the source papers profiles the original material.
